The compliance record’s own definitions

As the authors of the compliance record wrote them, in the file this page is built from. Where they mention the tool list or a list of pages read, those are parts of the record this site does not publish.

One row for each hosted product group in a tool-list entry's data and compliance block (a "For …" line there naming a product), so an entry whose block has two such groups has two rows; one mark per compliance item, from what the maker's own pages state, each linked to the page behind it. Not a compliance determination: read the linked page before relying on a mark. An empty mark means no current statement of the item was taken for this product from what was read, and the cell's blank gives the status word of the line that left it empty. It never means the item does not hold. The full data and compliance block for each tool, with retention, training use and residency, is in the tool list. For HIPAA and GDPR a mark reads only the maker's own claim that its product complies with or supports the law: a definition of the law, a bare badge or a data processing addendum never marks either. On the GDPR and HIPAA columns the word comes from a reading of the maker's claim alone, which the tool list prints as no line of its own, so it can differ from the word on the tool list's GDPR or HIPAA BAA line. For HIPAA, a page read for the tool list's HIPAA BAA line counts as read for the claim, because that line shows the HIPAA sentences taken from the page, not only BAA offers. HIPAA BAA is marked Y only where the maker offers a Business Associate Agreement, and No where its page places the product outside one. A plan, deployment option or region shown in quotation marks is the maker's own name for it, and the marks are part of the value: keep them wherever the value is shown. A row resting on pages that speak for another product, whether or not another entry holds it, is not confirmed by being listed: its product names whose marks it carries.

Y
the maker's page states the item for this product; with companyWide, shown as Y†, the mark rests instead on a sentence the maker files under the company, naming no product, and the page does not say whether its scope includes this product
Y*
the maker's page states the item only for the plans, deployment options or regions named in plans, or only under the conditions named in conditions, or both; where a cell names both, the matrix does not say whether they apply together: the tool list's line for this item shows each plan and condition beside the page it comes from
†
set on a mark resting on a sentence the maker files under the company, naming no product, whether or not it is limited to a plan, deployment option, region or condition: the page does not say whether its scope includes this product, so confirm the scope on the page (shown as Y†, as Y*† on a mark that also names plans or conditions, or as No† on an exclusion). A sentence whose own words reach every product the maker offers carries no companyWide
No
the maker's page states that the item does not apply here
Blank
no current statement of the item was taken for this product from what was read, and the cell's blank gives the status word of the line that left it empty. It never means the item does not hold. The words blank can carry, one sentence each: NOT-READ means no page of a kind that could answer it, and no search for one, is recorded as read for it for this product. NOT-STATED means a page of a kind that could answer it, or a search of the maker's site for one, was read for it, and no reviewed sentence answering it for this product was taken from it; a sentence that marks nothing, such as one describing the item as in progress, one naming it with no status, one about the law, or one naming a withdrawn revision, may sit under that line in the tool list. UNREADABLE means a page of a kind that could answer it, read for it, returned no readable text to the fetch used, and it is given even where another page read for it could be read and gave no answer, so it does not mean nothing was checked; the regulated-data statements list each maker's pages read and whether each could be read, and a person with a browser may be able to read the unreadable one. GATED means a page of a kind that could answer it, read for it, sits behind a login, an NDA request or a sales conversation, so nothing on it was read, and like UNREADABLE it is given even where another page read for it could be read. BLANK means the maker's own table leaves this cell empty. CONFLICTING means the sentences taken for it disagree. SALES-ONLY means the maker's page sends the question to a sales conversation. OPERATOR means the answer belongs to whoever runs the software.
Restriction
A restriction is a sentence on the maker's own page that places this product, or one plan, arrangement or feature of it, outside something a column asks about or outside zero data retention, or that bars using it where one of those items applies. It is not a mark and never a No: each cell keeps its own mark, and a restriction holds only for the scope shown with it, the condition, plans or arrangement it names. An item's condition and plans come from one sentence, so where it names both, it holds only where one of those plans and that condition apply at once. It is listed only where no column for the same item already reads No. The list covers the items in the columns and the zero-retention option, so it is not a full account of a maker's terms, and an empty list means none was recorded here, not that none exists. Beneath an item, also lists what else the tool list's line for that item shows that bears on it: another cell of the same row of the maker's table, or a mark the maker's own table gives the same item for this product that no column reads. Each carries its own page, section and read date, with a reviewed summary or, for a cell holding only a mark, the line the tool list generates from it; an empty also does not mean the maker says nothing more.

A tool resting on several products passes the filter when they carry the checked items between them. For where customer data goes, model by model, see customer data by cloud.

Why it shows nothing, in the record’s own words

Entries run by their operator (open-source software and model weights) are not listed: whoever runs them answers these questions for their own deployment. Where the maker also runs the same software as a hosted service, that hosted tier is a separate product and gets its own row once one of the maker's pages establishing it has been read; until then it has no row, and its absence says nothing about it. Also not listed: entries whose block rests on the same pages as an entry listed here, where a read of those pages found part of that record outside the entry. How to read a signal, on anything

These keep working on tools that never appear here.

Stars. Attention accumulated over a repository's whole life, so age is part of what it measures: an older project out-stars a better younger one by default. Nothing prompts anybody to un-star a project they stopped using.

Last push. When a commit last reached the default branch. A bot bumping a dependency and a rewrite of the scheduler are the same date here, and a project whose real work happens on release branches looks quieter than it is.

Archived. The owner set a flag, which is a fact rather than an inference. What it means is ambiguous: a project that moved to a new organization leaves a tombstone identical to one that stopped. Check whether the same name is alive somewhere else before concluding anything.

The license, twice. What the host reports and what is actually in the tree are not always the same answer. For anything you copy into your own repository, the directory you copy is the unit to check, at the revision you copy it from.

Open issues. The count rises with adoption and falls with triage policy, so a low number means few problems, or no queue, or a bot closing them on a timer. A signal with opposite readings is not a signal until you know which regime you are in.

And read the date, never the interval. Somebody else's "updated three months ago" was computed once, and it goes wrong while every input to it stays right. Do the subtraction yourself, against today.
