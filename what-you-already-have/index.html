These are pieces of infrastructure a request passes through. They are a different cut from the ownership tiers in who owns the answer, which are about who is answerable for what a component says about itself. Both get called layers and they are not the same list.

Two rows are missing, and both absences are claims

There is no row for evaluating what an agent produced. Not because it does not matter, but because nothing worth pointing at fills it honestly yet, and putting a row there would sell you a gap as a layer.

There is no row for a registry of prompts, contexts or agent definitions. One credible option is not a market, and a layer with one occupant is a decision you make once rather than a category you shop in.

How to read a signal, on anything

Whatever you end up looking at, you will be on a repository page deciding something from numbers. Numbers persuade without arguing. Each of these says what it measures, what it does not, and what moves it for reasons that have nothing to do with the tool.

Stars

MeasuresAttention, accumulated over the life of a repository. Somebody saw it and bookmarked it.

Does not measureQuality, fitness for your case, whether the code runs, or how many people use it in anger. A star is free and permanent, and nobody un-stars a project they stopped using.

Moves for unrelated reasonsAge, above all. A repository that has existed for five years out-stars a better one that has existed for two, by default, without either project doing anything. Compare only between projects of similar age, and treat the comparison as weak even then.

Last push

MeasuresThe most recent moment a commit reached the default branch, as the host reports it.

Does not measureWhether the project works, whether anybody answers issues, or whether what landed was substantial. A dependency bump from a bot and a rewrite of the scheduler are the same date here.

Moves for unrelated reasonsAutomated dependency updates, documentation typos and CI configuration all refresh it. A project whose real work happens on release branches will look quieter than it is.

Archived

MeasuresExactly one thing: the owner set the archived flag. That is the vendor's own declaration, and it is a fact rather than an inference.

Does not measureThe state of the project. A project that moved to a new organization leaves a tombstone identical to the one left by a project that stopped.

Moves for unrelated reasonsNothing moves it except a person deciding to set it. Which is why the reading is ambiguous rather than the signal being noisy: check whether the same name is alive somewhere else before you conclude anything.

The license, read two ways

MeasuresWhat the host reports, and what is actually in the tree. They are not always the same answer.

Does not measureWhether a subdirectory you are about to copy carries different terms from the root. For anything you vendor into your own repository, the directory you copy is the unit to check, at the revision you copy it from.

Moves for unrelated reasonsA relicense, a re-export, or a commercial edition walled off from the one you read about. The two answers disagreeing is a question for a person, never a verdict.

Open issues

MeasuresHow many issues are currently open. That is all.

Does not measureAnything about the project on its own, because it rises with adoption and falls with triage policy. A low number means few reported problems, or nobody routes reports into a queue, or a bot closes them on a timer.

Moves for unrelated reasonsA signal with two opposite readings is not a signal until you know which regime you are in. Read a few of the issues themselves: whether recent ones have maintainer replies, and whether closures carry a commit or a template. That takes less time than the arguing that follows a wrong reading.

And read the date, never the interval. Somebody else's "updated three months ago" was computed once, and it goes wrong while every input to it stays right: nobody pushes, nobody edits the sentence, and the sentence becomes false on its own. Find the date and do the subtraction yourself, against today.
